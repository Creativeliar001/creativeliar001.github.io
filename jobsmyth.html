Jobsmyth

An AI job search platform that finds roles a person can legally take, confirms each one is still open, and prepares application documents from their real experience only. I founded it and own it end to end.

Founder & Product Lead, sole product owner
Timeline

01

The problem

Job seekers lose hours to listings that are already closed, roles they can't legally take, and AI writing tools that make things up.

Aggregator listings often stay up long after an employer has filled a role. For international students and anyone whose work authorization matters, many of the openings they see are not open to them at all. And most AI resume tools optimize for sounding impressive, which is how invented numbers and skills end up in front of hiring managers.

Jobsmyth started from a simple question: what would a job search assistant look like if every suggestion could be trusted?

02

Product principles

Three rules guide every feature. They are also the promise on the Jobsmyth homepage.

Only open roles

Every match is confirmed open on the employer's own careers page or hiring system.

Only jobs you can take

Matches respect where the user lives, their work authorization, visa needs, and remote eligibility.

Nothing made up

Documents use only facts from the user's own resume and notes. The user reviews everything and applies themselves.

03

What I built

Three AI agents, each with one job.

  1. Job finder. Searches employer career sites daily for confirmed-open roles, then filters every job by work authorization, visa sponsorship, and remote eligibility before it reaches the user.
  2. Fit scorer. Scores fit from any posting link, so users can check a role they found themselves, not only the ones Jobsmyth suggests.
  3. Company researcher. Researches employers and cites its sources, so users can verify what they read before an interview.

Around the agents, Jobsmyth grew into a full application workflow: tailored resumes and cover letters, application tracking, outreach, and interview prep.

04

Building trust into the product

For a job seeker, one invented number on a resume can cost an offer. I treated truthfulness as a product requirement, not a prompt instruction, and designed three safeguards:

  • Real facts only. Generated documents draw only on the user's own resume and notes.
  • Unsupported numbers are flagged. If a figure isn't in the source material, the user sees it called out instead of quietly included.
  • Hidden instructions are ignored. Job postings can contain hidden text aimed at AI tools. Jobsmyth ignores instructions embedded in postings, a defense against prompt injection.
05

Feedback and continuous improvement

I launched as a small private pilot with 8 NC A&T job seekers, so I could learn from real searches before opening it to more people.

Users can report a posting that turns out to be expired. Those reports feed a self-improving search loop: a source that collects 3 expired-posting reports within 60 days is dropped automatically. The product gets more accurate the more people use it, without manual cleanup.

10+ releases in 4 weeks, as sole product owner.

Short release cycles let me move Jobsmyth from job matching into the rest of the application workflow, one tested step at a time.
